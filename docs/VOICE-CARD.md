# The Voice Card

Claude will build whatever you describe. The build is the easy part. The hard part is the three decisions the build depends on, and Claude cannot make them for you. If you let it, you get the average LinkedIn account.

Fill this in on paper before you open the first prompt. Ten minutes.

---

## 1. What you want to be known for

One line. Not a job title. The thing a stranger should be able to say about you after three posts.

Worked example: *Applied AI visibility as a designer. Someone who builds with AI every day and explains it to designers from the build, not the textbook.*

Yours:

> 

Everything below is measured against this line. A post type that does not serve it goes.

---

## 2. Post types

Three to eight. Each one has a shape and a purpose. Purpose is one of: reach, keeping the audience warm, conversion.

If you have never posted, start with three. The list grows once you have posted a few times and seen what gets replies.

The four below are a starting point, the kind of set most people write down first. They are seeded to unstick you, not to be chosen from. A better type that is not listed is a better type. Expect your first set to change after Claude has read your posts; it usually takes two or three passes to lock.

| Type | Shape | Purpose | Your version |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Field note | A thing seen or heard at work this week, and what it means | Reach | |
| Pattern | A recurring shape across projects, named | Reach and debate | |
| Lesson | Something learned, with the mistake that taught it | Reach | |
| Studio note | What is happening in the side practice | Conversion | |

Questions to ask of every type on your list:

- Which one is the pathway to the thing you are building? Give it room. Do not cut it to one idea.
- Which one is an audience, not a type? An audience can be carried by every post. It does not need its own column.
- Which one belongs to a project you cannot give prominence to yet? Cut it until you can.
- Which two are the same thing with different names? Merge them, or split them into what you actually write.
- Which one gets the most comments? If it is not on the list, it should be.
- What have you done for ten years that is not here? The type you forget is usually the one you are most qualified to write.

## 3. Naming rules

Write down which names can appear in a post. Claude will name everything unless told.

| | Rule | Yours |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Past employers | Never named. Described by shape: "a lending startup where I was the first design hire", "a security company with 13 designers across 58 markets". | |
| Current employer | Only where the post makes no sense without it, and only on settled work. | |
| Clients | Never. Sector only. | |
| Side projects and studios | Not while employed, unless the post is about them and that is allowed. | |
| Colleagues | Named for what they did well, never for performance. | |
| Topics off the table | Job search, employer strategy, resignation, burnout as your own story. | |

## 4. Where it lands

| | Worked example | Yours |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Approved posts | A Craft table "Content schedule": name, type, status, shortlist, date, hook, second line, body, alternative openings | |
| Proposed ideas | A Craft page per week, "Ideas, week of <date>", one section per idea | |
| Meetings | Granola, last seven days | |
| Long-form drafts | Inside the idea's own section on the weekly page, as a toggle | |

Pages beat tables for anything you will read. Tables beat pages for anything you will sort.

Leave Meetings blank if you have no meeting notes tool. The weekly loop has a fallback. If a spreadsheet is all you have, approved posts go to a sheet and the weekly ideas become rows instead of a page.

---

## 5. Voice, the short version

You do not write this. Claude reads your posts and interviews you, then writes it as a skill. But you should know what a good one contains, so you can tell when yours is thin.

A voice skill worth keeping has: an interview step before any drafting; the shape of a post in your terms (paragraphs, or lines, or whatever you do); the four or five ways you open, taken from your own posts; how you end, taken from your own essays or talks; the words and phrases you never use; one reference post per type, written by you; a list of drafts you rejected and why.

The last item is the one that compounds. Every time you say "I would never say this", the rejected line goes in the skill.

---

## Why this is the whole workshop

The build takes twenty minutes and Claude does it. The three decisions above take ten minutes and only you can make them. Everyone who skips the card ends up with a page that writes good posts for someone who is not them.
